# Product

## Register

product

## Users

First-time mobile app creators preparing for an App Store or Google Play launch: indie React Native, Flutter, and no-code builders, and solo founders shipping an MVP. They are technically capable of building but feel overwhelmed by launch prep, scared of missing something important, and unsure what to do next. They use the app in short, anxious bursts (often late at night, often alone) and need to feel guided rather than tested.

The job to be done is emotional before it is functional: they are buying relief, confidence, and a sense of "I am not missing anything." On any given screen the primary task is "show me the next move and let me make progress on it without thinking about the whole mountain."

## Product Purpose

LaunchDeckAI is a guided launch clarity system for app creators, not a generic project manager. It turns launch confusion into launch readiness through AI coaching (Astro), guided Blueprints, launch Missions and milestones, AI asset generation (Foundry), an asset vault (Cargo Bay), and a 16-step promotional Signal Deck.

Success looks like a user who can sign up, create a Mission, complete onboarding, see a launch readiness score, follow milestones, fill Blueprints, generate and save assets, prepare Signal Deck content, ask Astro for guidance, and upgrade from Cadet to Commander or Admiral, all while feeling steadily more prepared instead of more overwhelmed.

## Brand Personality

Calm mission control with handcrafted warmth. Three words: **calm, capable, companionable.**

The voice is the steady flight director in your ear, never the drill sergeant and never the cheerleader. It is confident and expert without being cold, warm and encouraging without being saccharine or childish. Astro is a character, an emotional anchor, not a gimmick. Every interaction should lower the user's heart rate, not raise it. The launch-world metaphor (Deck, Missions, Fuel, Signal, Cargo Bay, Refuel) is the connective tissue that makes the product memorable and makes marketing feel concrete instead of vague.

Emotional goals: relief, preparedness, quiet confidence, feeling supported. Anxiety reduction is a design requirement, not a nicety.

## Anti-references

- **Sterile checklist tool.** A plain to-do list with no character, no warmth, no metaphor, no companion. Stripping out Astro and the launch-world identity would gut the product. Progress must feel like momentum on a mission, not boxes ticked on a list.
- **Corporate SaaS dashboard.** Flat gray cards, dense data tables, enterprise-cool detachment, the hero-metric template (big number + small label + stat row + gradient accent). Too cold and too generic for an anxiety-reducing companion aimed at scared solo founders.
- Also avoid: childish gamification (cartoon mascots, candy colors, confetti spam) that undermines expert credibility, and the generic dark crypto/AI reflex (neon-on-black, purple gradients, glowing grid lines).

## Design Principles

1. **Relief over information.** Reduce anxiety first. Show the next move, not the whole mountain. When in doubt, surface less and reassure more.
2. **Calm confidence, never cold.** Read as an expert flight director: composed, precise, warm. No drill sergeant, no cheerleader, no enterprise detachment.
3. **The metaphor earns its keep.** Deck, Fuel, Signal, Missions, Cargo Bay are a coherent world, not decoration. Use it to make abstract launch work feel concrete and navigable, never as costume over a generic to-do app.
4. **Earn the signature moments.** Fuel earned, milestone complete, signal ready, launch day. These rare moments deserve craft and delight; everything between them stays quiet so they land.
5. **Progress you can feel.** Every screen should make momentum visible (readiness ring, signal bars, fuel) so a frightened first-timer always sees they are moving forward.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target **WCAG 2.1 AA** contrast across text and meaningful UI.
- **Honor reduced motion.** The app is intentionally motion-rich (starfield, orbital transitions, glow pulses); respect `prefers-reduced-motion` / the OS reduce-motion setting and degrade to calm static or cross-fade equivalents.
- Minimum **44px tap targets**.
- **Never rely on color alone** for status. Pair color with icon, label, or shape (e.g. SignalBars fill count, readiness ring, fuel glyph).
- Support larger Dynamic Type where feasible; avoid text below 12px except decorative labels.
- Provide accessible labels for all icon-only controls.
- Keep copy clear and anxiety-reducing; plain language over jargon, reassurance over alarm.
